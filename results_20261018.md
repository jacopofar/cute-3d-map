# Feature experiments — 2026-10-18

Autonomous run: each feature was attempted on its own branch off `main`, one at a
time, with a 45-minute budget each. Nothing was merged: pick and merge what you
want. Tiles (`public/tiles/`) and `data/` are gitignored, so every tilegen
change needs a tile regeneration to be seen (command per section).

Baseline (`main`, this machine, SwiftShader): startup 49 tiles / 178 meshes /
456,869 drawn triangles / 25 draw calls; idle p50 165.4 ms, pan p50 146.6 ms,
p90 181.4 ms. The box is shared and noisy — treat the numbers as rough.

Status: all seven features attempted; each is committed on its branch. See the
per-feature sections and the wrap-up at the end.

---

## 1. Benches — WORKED

- **Branch** `feature/benches`, commit `c61d6e4`.
- **What works**: `amenity=bench` becomes `cmtype: "bench"` (points, and the
  midpoint + direction of bench ways). The frontend builds one merged bench
  per feature — seat plank, backrest, four legs, wood/metal vertex colors — and
  places it facing the OSM `direction`, else a stable seeded facing. A
  `#benches-toggle` checkbox is a plain per-tile visibility switch. 12,729
  benches across 531 tiles; ~3,000 were loaded in the benchmark window.
- **Screenshot**: `docs/experiments_20261018/benches_closeup.png` (zoom ~21: reads clearly
  as a bench), `docs/experiments_20261018/benches_parco.png` (Parco Sempione, the small
  brown marks among the trees).
- **Still open / decisions**: benches are true-size (~1.6 m), so at zoom 18 they
  are a few pixels — enlarge them or zoom-gate them if you want them to read at
  a glance. Nothing else blocking.
- **Tests**: `tsc` clean; vitest 86 pass (new `bench.test.ts`); pytest 73 pass;
  e2e 9 pass (new `e2e/benches.spec.ts` counts `kind:"bench"` meshes and toggles
  them).
- **Perf** vs baseline: startup 224 meshes / 481,493 tris / 30 draw calls
  (baseline 178 / 456,869 / 25); idle p50 164.4 ms, pan p50 161 / p90 203.7
  (baseline 165.4 / 146.6 / 181.4) — benches add ~218k tris (~5%) and no
  draw-call blowup; the pan delta is mostly noise on this shared box.

## 2. Churches — WORKED

- **Branch** `feature/churches`, commit `dc8ab2b`.
- **What works**: tilegen now reports each building's `building` value and, for
  places of worship, `religion`. `isChurch()` (new `src/elements3d/church.ts`)
  classifies a church from those tags. A church whose color OSM left out takes a
  seeded stone/ochre wall + slate roof, gets a **steep gabled roof**
  (`CHURCH_PITCH` 1.3x, when the footprint can be pitched at all), and a **gold
  cross** on the ridge, placed from the same `roofPlan` the roof uses so it sits
  on the ridge instead of floating. The cross is its own per-tile kind
  (`church`), so `#churches-toggle` hides just the ornament and leaves the walls
  to the building layer. 795 features classify as churches.
- **Screenshot**: `docs/experiments_20261018/churches_sacro_cuore.png` (hero: stone walls,
  steep slate gable, gold cross on the ridge), plus
  `churches_sant_ambrogio.png` / `churches_santa_maria_delle_grazie.png` for
  street contrast and `churches_duomo.png`.
- **Still open / decisions**: no bell tower (deliberately skipped — a cross
  alone was the ask and a tower would be invented geometry); flat-roofed
  churches (the Duomo, a concave/>8-corner footprint) keep their flat cap, so
  only the cross marks them. The church palette wins over an *imputed* color even
  when "imputed colors" is unchecked (it is our decoration, not sampled data) —
  flag if you disagree.
- **Tests**: `tsc` clean; vitest 94 pass (new `church.test.ts`, includes
  cross-on-ridge geometry); pytest 68 pass; e2e 9 pass (new `e2e/church.spec.ts`).
- **Perf** vs baseline: startup 221 meshes / 456,997 tris / 27 draw calls;
  idle p50 160.9, pan p50 145 — negligible (church kind is ~2.3k tris).

## 3. Parks & grass — WORKED

- **Branch** `feature/parks`, commit `f8f9938`.
- **What works**: tilegen emits OSM green areas as `cmtype: "green"` with a
  `green` sub-kind, clipped to the tile like water (a park spans tiles) and
  split into a mown style (`grass`: landuse grass/village_green/meadow/
  recreation_ground, natural grass/grassland, leisure garden/pitch) and a dense
  one (`dense`: leisure park/common/nature_reserve, natural wood/scrub/heath,
  landuse forest/orchard). Farmland is left out on purpose; areas under 40 m²
  (`MIN_GREEN_AREA`) are dropped. The `elif is_green` branch sits after water, so
  a wetland that is also `landuse=grass` stays water.
  The new `src/elements3d/green.ts` lays the polygons flat at z = -0.02 (under
  the water at 0, over the ground at -0.05) and shades them with one
  `ShaderMaterial`: a per-vertex `aStyle` picks mown stripes (fixed world
  direction, `fwidth`-faded so a zoomed-out view does not moire) or a mottled
  three-octave noise in deeper greens; the per-feature base tint (fresh green
  vs deep green) keeps neighbours from looking identical. One mesh per tile,
  `#parks-toggle` is a plain visibility switch.
- **Numbers**: 22,830 green features in 1,110 of 1,427 tiles (dense in 1,059
  tiles, grass in 962). In the benchmark window: 49 green meshes, 69,213
  vertices, 23,071 triangles.
- **Screenshot**: `docs/experiments_20261018/parks_sempione_v3.png` (hero: Parco Sempione,
  both styles and the pond clearly distinct), plus `parks_milano_v2.png`
  (zoom 15, city greens subdued) and `parks_guastalla_v2.png`.
- **Still open / decisions**: none blocking. Farmland exclusion is the main
  judgement call; the 40 m² threshold can be raised if the tile payload matters.
- **Tests**: `tsc` clean; vitest 86 pass (new `green.test.ts`); e2e 9 pass (new
  `e2e/green.spec.ts` counts `kind:"green"` meshes and toggles `#parks-toggle`);
  pytest 72 pass, coverage 86%; ruff + ty clean.
- **Perf** vs baseline: startup 227 meshes / 458,989 tris / 31 draw calls
  (baseline 178 / 456,869 / 25); idle p50 141.1, p90 158.7, pan p50 148.5,
  p90 172.7 (baseline idle 165.4 / 184.6, pan 146.6 / 181.4). The green kind is
  ~23k tris (~0.5%): negligible.

## 4. Airplanes — WORKED (one cosmetic call)

- **Branch** `feature/airplanes`, commit `f1572f4`.
- **What works**: tilegen emits `cmtype: "aeroway"` for runways/taxiways (lines,
  width from the `width` tag else 45/15 m) and aprons/helipads (areas); runways
  are stored whole per tile and deduped by id. The frontend draws the tarmac as
  flat ribbons/areas (kind `aeroway`) and, over it, a `PlaneLayer` (like
  `CloudLayer`/`TrafficRenderer`) of shared low-poly airliners. Each runway has a
  seeded, **stateless** schedule: a flight is visible for part of a 60–84 s
  period as a takeoff (line up, roll, rotate, climb out) or a landing; direction
  alternates. Planes are gated below zoom 16. `#airplanes-toggle` hides tarmac +
  planes.
- **Screenshot**: `docs/experiments_20261018/airplanes_linate.png` — an airliner mid-roll on
  the Linate runway (the suggested center sits over the terminal; the runway is
  ~500 m east, at `16/45.4452/9.2767`).
- **Still open / decisions**: the tarmac reads **too dark** — a heavy near-black
  band over MapTiler's own light runway (`#3a3d42` is near-black once tone
  mapped). Easy knobs: lighten `AEROWAY_COLORS`, narrow the ribbon, or drop the
  surface entirely and keep only the planes (relying on the basemap runway). I
  left it as-is rather than spend the budget; it is the one thing I would change.
  Landing flights have no taxiway path, so they vanish after roll-out; bare-node
  helipads are skipped.
- **Tests**: `tsc` clean; vitest 84 pass (new `airplanes.test.ts`); pytest 72
  pass, 86%; e2e 9 pass (new `e2e/airplanes.spec.ts` jumps to Linate and toggles
  the surface). `npm run bench` essentially unchanged (49 tiles / 180 meshes /
  456,869 tris / 25 calls; idle p50 140.5, pan p50 142.2).

## 5. Day and night — WORKED

- **Branch** `feature/day-night`, commit `634cfe8`. Frontend only — no tilegen
  change, no tile regeneration needed.
- **What works**: a pure `lightingAt(hour)` (`src/daynight.ts`) maps 0–24 h to
  sun/fill/hemisphere color+intensity, exposure and a 0–1 `night` factor (warm
  dawn/dusk horizon, dim cool moon at night); `main.ts` keeps the light
  references and applies it. UI: `#night-toggle` (unchecked 13:00, checked
  01:00) plus a `#time-slider` (0–24 h, step 0.25) driving the same model.
  Windows carry a seeded per-window `aLight` attribute (~34% lit per building,
  ~8% of buildings fully dark — doors never light) and the windows group got its
  own `windowMaterial` shader with a `uNight` uniform, so **some** windows glow
  warm amber at night, not all. The basemap swaps to MapTiler `basic-v2-dark` at
  night (verified, `setStyle` keeps the custom layer); clouds and water dim too;
  exposure/water/cloud tints follow the cycle.
- **Screenshot**: `docs/experiments_20261018/night_street.png` (dark basemap, scattered warm
  windows, some buildings dark) vs `docs/experiments_20261018/day_street.png`; also
  `night_duomo.png` / `night_clouds.png` (zoom 12, the basemap/clouds).
- **Still open / decisions**: the sun path is a stylized arc (no date/season);
  the basemap swap is a hard switch at `night = 0.5` while the lighting fades
  smoothly — a crossfade or a season/latitude parameter is the obvious follow-up.
- **Tests**: `tsc` clean; vitest 91 pass (`daynight.ts` 100% stmts); e2e 9 pass
  (new night assertion: `night` 0→1, sun intensity >2 → <0.5, windows `uNight`
  0→1, checkbox/slider in sync). `npm run bench` identical to baseline (178
  meshes / 456,869 tris / 25 calls; idle p50 139.7, pan p50 141.4).

## 6. Fences — WORKED

- **Branch** `feature/fences`, commit `bcc3afc`.
- **What works**: tilegen emits `cmtype: "fence"` for `barrier` in
  `{fence, guard_rail, railing}` line features (a polygon barrier contributes its
  outline), clipped to the tile like water and with sub-metre slivers dropped;
  18,263 fence features in 786 tiles. The frontend builds a thin two-sided
  vertical band (1.15 m) carrying `aU` (distance along) and `aZ` (height), and a
  `ShaderMaterial` carves **three rails + posts on a 2 m pitch by discarding**
  the gaps — so it reads as a fence, not a wall. Wood brown for `fence`, metal
  grey for `guard_rail`/`railing`. `#fences-toggle` is a visibility switch.
- **Screenshot**: `docs/experiments_20261018/fences_naviglio.png` (zoom 20: posts + rails
  with gaps along a canal-side boundary fence), plus `fences_buildings.png` in
  context.
- **Still open / decisions**: visibility is gated at zoom 18 (`FENCE_MIN_ZOOM`) —
  legibility, not perf: at 17 a 1.1 m band is ~1 px and looks dashed; the gate
  is a one-line change to 17. Fences are not click-pickable. The pixel cost is
  tiny (a zoom-18 probe showed pan p50 54.2 ms with fences on vs 54.7 off).
- **Tests**: `tsc`/`build` clean; vitest 89 pass (new `fence.test.ts`); pytest 72
  pass, 85.7%; e2e 8 pass (fences counted and toggled at zoom 18). `npm run
  bench` unchanged (227 meshes but fences gated off at the bench zoom; 456,869
  tris, 25 calls; idle p50 139.8, pan p50 146).

## 7. Bridges and tunnels — WORKED (partial by nature)

- **Branch** `feature/bridges-tunnels`, commit `7ae91d5`.
- **What works**: tilegen emits `cmtype: "bridge"` for `bridge=*` ways on a
  highway/railway and for `man_made=bridge` / `building=bridge` footprints
  (clipped like water), carrying `layer` + class; and `cmtype: "tunnel"` as a
  **Point at each true tunnel end**. The frontend raises a bridge way into a
  slab (`h = max(4, 4.5·layer)`, class width, parapets, **both ends ramped to
  the ground over 15 m**) and a footprint into a flat deck; a tunnel end gets a
  dark **portal** frame. `building=bridge` no longer gets a building roof/facade
  (a known wart, fixed). One `#bridges-toggle` visibility switch covers both
  kinds.
- **Screenshot**: `docs/experiments_20261018/bridge_final.png` (Cavalcavia Maggi: two raised
  decks swooping over the streets, ends ramped, a portal underneath),
  `bridge_regen.png` (deck over the Naviglio), `tunnel_final.png` (rail-throat
  portals).
- **Still open / decisions**: the underground part of a tunnel is **not drawn on
  purpose** — only the portals. Portals are restricted to road/rail classes:
  the extract's tunnels are mostly footway/steps underpasses and drawing them
  all buried the scene (≥227k verts, 10× duplicate frames). Clustered portals at
  a rail throat are not deduped. A `man_made=bridge` deck's sides drop to the
  ground (no end ramp, unlike line bridges) and can read as a pier wall over
  water. Bridges are not click-pickable. Bridges/tunnels are not zoom-gated.
- **Tests**: `tsc` clean; vitest 89 pass (`bridge_layout.ts` 97%); pytest 77
  pass, 85.6%; e2e 10 pass (toggle hides both kinds; a tunnel end draws a
  portal). `npm run bench`: 244 meshes / 458,420 tris / 28 calls; bridge 13,560
  tri + tunnel 17,676 tri in the bench view; idle p50 123.8, pan p50 141.1.

## Wrap-up

All seven features are on their own branch off `main`; none are merged. Every
one adds a layers-menu checkbox and passed `tsc`, unit, e2e and the benchmark.
The main caveats across the set:

- **Tiles are gitignored.** Each branch changed tilegen, so to see a feature you
  must check the branch out and regenerate:
  `cd tilegen && uv run main.py ../data/milano_20261016.pbf --polygon-filter
  ../data/milano_polygon.json --output ../public/tiles --no-raw` (~60 s).
  After merging branches you will have to resolve conflicts in
  `tilegen/main.py`, `src/geojson_data.ts`, `src/main.ts` and `index.html` (each
  feature edits the same dispatch chain / checkbox list), then regenerate once.
- Each branch only contains its own feature: the branches are independent off
  `main`, so the tiles on disk right now hold the *bridges-tunnels* build only.
- The two cosmetic calls I would revisit first: the **airport tarmac is too
  dark** (feature 4), and **benches are true-size** so they are tiny at zoom 18
  (feature 1). Fences are gated at zoom 18 and bridges/tunnels have no picking.
